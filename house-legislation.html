How a bill becomes law — and where the traps hide

Most bills die quietly in committee. The few that move pass through stages, and watching the status tells you how close a measure is to becoming binding law: Introduced → In committee → Reported → Passed House → Passed Senate → To the President → Law.

Phrases the detector watches for

Legislation has a vocabulary for hiding power, cost and consequences. A few of the big ones:

"notwithstanding any other provision of law" — quietly overrides every other law that might limit a provision.
"such sums as may be necessary" — authorizes spending with no dollar cap.
"in the sole discretion of the Secretary" — hands one official broad, often unreviewable power.
"shall not be subject to judicial review" — strips the courts' ability to check the action.
"is amended by striking … inserting …" — changes existing law by reference, so the real effect is invisible unless you read it against current law.

About this tool. Bill data comes live from the official Library of Congress / Congress.gov API and bill text from congress.gov. Nothing here is editorialized at the source — the words are Congress's own. The trap & vague-verbiage detector uses transparent keyword rules to flag language that commonly hides cost, power, or consequences; it highlights things worth reading carefully and is not an accusation of wrongdoing. The optional AI deep-dive is generated by a language model and can make mistakes — always read the bill text yourself.
